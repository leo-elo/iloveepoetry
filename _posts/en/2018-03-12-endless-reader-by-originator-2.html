This application is the continuation of "Endless Alphabet," which integrates "sight words" with an interactive digital environment in order to allow children to hear words broken down into their simplest phonetic segments. For example, in the word "all," the individual sounds of "a" and "l" are shown. By doing this, children can see the function of each of these segments and then see how these segments interact when they combine to form a complete word. The main goal of this application is to provide children with an advantage in their education in an entertaining and colorful way, as can be seen in the introductory sequence below. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goKbaHzdNY8 When the application is launched, a group of colorful and funny monsters appears on the screen, holding and carrying the letters of the title of the work, "Endless Reader." They carry individual words and suffixes, depending on the meaning of the individual word. What is interesting about the opening is that it gives the application user a preview of the app itself. The child will be exposed to a series of short words, prepositions, suffixes, and other parts of speech. The monsters will help the child learn to use sight words on their own and in complete sentences. The start menu of "Endless Reader" shows the words in alphabetical order. This, in turn, references the previous application in the series and presents new ways to apply what users have learned. Each word tells a story. The monsters carry out an action that metaphorically represents the word being studied. Here we have the word "funny," which is displayed on a touchscreen device, where the monsters pass over it and separate the word into letters. When the user touches a letter, it produces the phonetic segment of each letter. The letter itself transforms into a mini monster with facial features, creating the allusion of a person modeling the sound. Once the word is completed, the user moves on to the next stage of the game, where the word is used in a complete sentence. Here, the movements and gestures of the words tell the user that "funny" is associated with other terms and expressions like "smiling" and happiness. Ready for endless entertainment in the next-generation reading app for kids? If you are looking for a way to educate your children in a manner that enhances their digital literacy, then this app is for you. Originator's website offers more information about this application and its counterparts, which are available on iOS and Android.
