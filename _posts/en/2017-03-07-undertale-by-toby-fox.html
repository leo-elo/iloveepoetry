La manera que Toby Fox revisa las mecánicas y topos tradicionales..." permalink: "/en/aural/undertale-by-toby-fox/" wp_post_id: 12699 translation: "/es/aural-es/undertale-por-toby-fox/" featured_image: "/assets/images/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Undertale-Banner.png" work_url: "https://undertale.com/" --- At first glance, Undertale might strike the reader as another cheap homage to the retro era of video games, but this indie game does much more with its minimalist aesthetic than most AAA games achieve with their large budgets. The way Toby Fox revises traditional mechanics and tropes to tell us about a child trying to find the way home is a skillful union of content and form that uses its game system to reinforce its themes of pacifism and perspective. To be clear, its aesthetic is retro, and for those too young to know the precursors to modern RPG graphics, such as Moria (1994) and Adventure (1979), its art might be perceived as lazy rather than inspired. Likewise, its music is similar to that of Earthbound, and its progression and minimalism are part of what make the game moving: certain dramatic scenes in Undertale will make the player feel the absence of music as much as its presence. But that is the great thing about Undertale -- it is doing so much with so little: challenging stereotypes and literary tropes present in video games that we take for granted, while walking us down memory lane. Undertale delivers a charming story with a few eccentric twists, breaks the fourth wall, and stays interesting by incorporating other familiar game styles from the arcade era into its traditional JRPG combat system. The story challenges your sense of where the game begins and ends: for example, (minor spoiler) the first time you think you have found a programming bug and restart Undertale only to find the villain ready to attack you on the start screen, it will leave you smiling at its creativity. Its RPG mechanics continue to charm you. From simple moments like consuming "instant noodles" to recover (a process that takes a total of about 40 seconds in a fast-paced game), to a dodging mechanic that features dozens of mini-games imitating everything from Space Invaders to Frogger. But its battle system is more than a retro reference: at the heart of this game is a challenge to the common associations between heroism and violence, and the only way to reach the true ending of the game is to use all your energy to avoid violence whenever possible. Undertale is a testament to how making video game creation systems accessible contributes to the indie market. Its engine, GameMaker: Studio, is one of many programs that enrich the video game market by making the game development process more manageable for small companies and individuals like Toby Fox. Toby Fox created this game by himself, except for some design help received from Temmie Chang. Undertale is a game that understands the form and context of RPGs so well that the moment it breaks the agreements between players and games will impact you as vividly as a great poet manipulates meter in a verse to achieve a powerful effect. Its popularity is due to an audience that was raised in gaming culture to the point where people are literate enough in the genre to understand the complexity of many of the manipulations and references in Undertale, and the degrees of appreciation it has received from its players is a sign that gamer culture is ready to analyze texts of a greater sophistication than developers would have thought possible.
