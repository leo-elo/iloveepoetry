Sometimes it meows or purrs, sometimes it describes actions, such as "*leaves dissected animals on the front step*" and https://twitter.com/storyofglitch/status/476883799045709824 These tweets occur on a seemingly random timer, but you can always get a reaction by interacting with it. For example, if you follow it on Twitter, it will follow you. If you address it, it responds. https://twitter.com/storyofglitch/status/476886489565888512 So basically, it's a trés jolie chat bot. But there's more, because this isn't just randomly selecting interactions from a prewritten data set, as I discovered when reading this tweet from @thricedotted: https://twitter.com/thricedotted/status/476871530421702658 So there's more complexity to this bot than what seems when you simply read its output, which would be surprisingly diverse, even if we didn't know that the bot is powered by an API search of  tweets (probably by search terms "cat," "kitten," etc.) by people who share experiences of their cats on Twitter. https://twitter.com/thricedotted/status/476872307244531714 The emoji cat in this bot's name, "glitch[META] ~(=^‥^)," reinforces the human-mediated representation of this cat, all of which happen through language, be it typographical, onomatopoeic, descriptive, narrative, or poetic. And thanks to its data source, its behavior is just as unpredictable, surprising, charming, and cute as having a real cat. And fortunately, because we know cats are unable to type (at least not in a way that deliberately produces language :-), there won't be any nonsense about it passing the Turing Test. https://twitter.com/storyofglitch/status/477033502441881600 https://twitter.com/storyofglitch/status/477045507819200513 https://twitter.com/storyofglitch/status/476970765917511680 This bot is a fitting tribute to the true masters of the Internet: cat lovers.
