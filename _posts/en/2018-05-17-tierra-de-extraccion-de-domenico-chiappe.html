In Tierra de Extraccion (1996-2007) (ELC2), Domenico Chiappe's first hypermedial novel, the extraction of meaning is generated through interaction and manipulation. Poetry is hidden in the fissures of the earth that slide to create motion in the different multimedia layers of the work. The novel is composed of 63 hypermedial chapters, each of them represented by an interactive [key] word. Similar to Hotel Minotauro (2013-2014), Tierra de Extraccion is an example of interactive narrative. For instance, in one of the chapters (Mangal/Mango Tree), the reader is invited to learn how to roll a die interactively in order to unfold the stories that lie behind each of its faces. The interaction with the dice produces an empty staging to be fulfilled by aesthetic chance. Rolling the dice becomes the space where chance meets creation.

When exploring the novel, the reader creates non-renewable reading paths in the aesthetic fissures of the work, which lead to a mango tree that tears apart multimedia poetry lines. The mango tree feeds from memory; it feeds from the blood of those who perished during the Revolution or the construction of Zumaque I, the first major Venezuelan oil well. The idea of oil stains on memory is emphasized by the suggestive watercolors that accompany the interactive narrative. Once again, the technique of music guiding the plot can be appreciated, as there are times when the song being played tells a different story from the one being read or painted. Let us not forget that not all the multimedia layers of the work are activated at the same time. There is independence between interactivity and narrativity. As previously mentioned, at times a picture, a watercolor, or a song can narrate more than a thousand clicks. Memory games and the intertextuality of images play an important role in Chiappe's work. At times, navigation may be guided solely by visual memory and aural recollection. A selection of photographs and paintings of Menegrande from specific periods of the 20th century underline the richness of the different arts that compose the works' historical hyperphony. The author uses the metaphoric effect d'amont en aval (upstream and downstream) to show the importance of artistic cooperation behind an electronic literature work. One of the recurring figures of animation found in the narrative is cine-gramme (Saemmer, 2012), which is when a strong relation between what the text does and what it says is created. This can be observed in the chapter (Milagro!/Miracle!) where words fall into the fissures of the Earth, and then rise and evaporate from it as the narrative voice suggests: "A veces me parece que esta tierra se ha tragado para que otro la extraiga" (sometimes it seems to me as if this land has swallowed it so that another can extract it). Chiappe underlines the complex narrative cartography of the work by calling for action and direction by the reader who, by going North, South, East, or West, becomes a sailor that decides the pace and development of the plot. However, the navigation and extraction of meaning is not easy; the reader's choice of actions may lead to rich moments of aesthetics of frustration (Bootz, 2005). As written in one of the chapters of Tierra de Extraccion (Bus/Bus), the reader will learn that... "esta tierra tiene su encanto pero debes descubrirlo" (this land has its charm but you have to discover it).
